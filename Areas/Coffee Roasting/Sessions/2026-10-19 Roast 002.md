### Espresso dial-in — day 12 (2026-10-31)
Fixed: 11.5 g dose, single basket, auto-tamp. No visible channeling on the puck.

| # | Temp | Grind | Yield | Time | Taste |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 93 °C | base | 17 g | 25 s | Sour first, then bitter |
| 2 | 95 °C | base | 23 g | 28 s | Clearly more bitter → higher temp rejected |
| 3 | 93 °C | base | 24 g | 24 s | Good balance sour/bitter, but harsh; no chocolate or nut notes |
| 4 | 93 °C | 1 step finer | 24 g | 30 s | More flavour notes, but turns sour after a few seconds |
| 5 | 93 °C | 1 step finer | 28 g | 28 s | More bitter, not good. Ran faster than #4 despite more yield |

- **Findings:** 93 °C is better than 95 °C for this roast. Raising yield from 17 g to 24 g balanced sour and bitter. Finer grind (30 s) gave more notes but a sour aftertaste. Going to 28 g made it bitter: the usable window is narrow, around 24–25 g.
- **Best recipe so far:** 11.5 g / 24 g / ~30 s / 93 °C, finer grind (#4).
- **Assessment:** Sour and bitter sit very close together with no sweet middle. Points to the roast (possible baked roast, see section 4/6) rather than the espresso settings.
- **Note:** Chocolate/nut notes are not expected at this roast level; expect fruit/tea-like notes instead. Retest the same recipe after more rest.

## 6. Conclusion and next roast
- **What the change did:** Indirect heat removed the scorching. Evenness between beans is much better.
- **New problem:** Weak, sparse first crack plus mottled surfaces point to too little heat energy late in the browning phase. Weight loss 16.0 % shows the beans are not underdeveloped overall; the risk is a long, slow ("baked") roast with flat, papery taste rather than sour.
- **Keep / reject:** Keep indirect heat.
- **Next roast — change only this:** Raise grill temperature by ~15 °C (about 250 °C on the lid), target first crack ~10:30–11:30 with a more continuous crack, drop ~2:30–3:00 after first crack.
- **Measurement to add:** Probe at basket height, to know the real temperature vs. the lid thermometer.
