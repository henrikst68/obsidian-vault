# Roasting Guide — BBQ Rotisserie Drum

## 1. Equipment
| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Perforated stainless drum for the rotisserie spit | Holds the beans | Holes must be smaller than a bean (≤ 4 mm). Fill to max ~1/3 of drum volume so the beans can tumble. |
| Grill with lid and an oven thermometer / probe placed at drum height | Measures the air temperature around the drum | The lid thermometer is often far off. Put a probe next to the drum. |
| IR thermometer | Spot check of bean surface after dumping | Only indicative. |
| Kitchen scale (0.1 g if possible) | Green and roasted weight | Weight loss is your most reliable "degree of roast" number. |
| Two metal colanders + a fan, or a box fan with a sieve on top | Fast cooling | Beans must be below ~40 °C within 3–4 minutes. |
| Heat-proof gloves, tray | Dumping the hot drum | |
| Timer / phone | Timeline log | |
| Roast colour reference (printed, or photos of your own past roasts) | Visual comparison | Photograph in the same light every time. |

**Gas vs. charcoal:** Gas is much easier to repeat because you can set and read the burner position. Start on gas if you have the option. With charcoal, record the amount of charcoal and how long it burned before you started.

## 2. What happens in the roast
| Phase | What you see / hear / smell | Typical bean temp | Typical time (BBQ drum) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drying | Green → pale yellow, grassy / hay smell | up to ~150 °C | 0 – 6–9 min |
| Browning (Maillard) | Yellow → tan → light brown, bread / toast smell | ~150–195 °C | ends at first crack |
| First crack | Popping sound like popcorn, chaff flies, volume grows | ~195–205 °C | often 10–16 min |
| Development | From start of first crack to drop | | 15–25 % of total time |
| Second crack | Quieter, faster crackling (like rice cereal), oil starts to show, much smoke | ~220–230 °C | 3–5 min after first crack ends |

A BBQ drum heats mostly by hot air (convection) and some radiation from the burners. It is usually slower and less even than a shop roaster. A total time of 12–20 minutes is normal.

## 3. Starting point (baseline recipe)
Use this for your first 2–3 roasts. Do not change anything until you can repeat it.
- **Batch:** 300 g green beans (washed Central or South American coffee is forgiving: Brazil, Colombia, Guatemala).
- **Preheat:** Grill with drum running, lid closed, to **230–250 °C** at the drum for 10–15 min.
- **Charge:** Load beans, start the timer. The temperature will drop and then climb back.
- **Heat:** Keep the grill temperature steady. Aim for first crack at **11–14 min**.
- **After first crack starts:** Lower the heat a little (one burner step or open the lid a crack) so the cracking does not run away.
- **Drop:** For a medium roast, drop **1:30–2:30 min after first crack starts**, before any sign of second crack.
- **Cool:** Dump straight into the colander, stir under the fan, outside, until cool to the touch.
- **Weigh:** Expect **13–16 % weight loss** for a medium roast.

## 4. Key numbers to calculate every time
- **Weight loss % = (green g − roasted g) / green g × 100**
	- Light: ~11–13 % · Medium: ~13–16 % · Dark: ~16–20 %
- **Development time ratio (DTR) % = (drop time − first crack time) / drop time × 100**
	- 15–20 % brighter / more acidic · 20–25 % sweeter / rounder
- **Time to yellow** — shows if the drying phase was too fast or too slow.

## 5. How to experiment
1. **One variable per roast.** If you change both heat and batch size, you cannot tell which one caused the result.
2. **Keep a stable baseline.** Repeat the baseline until two roasts land within ±1 min on first crack and ±1 % on weight loss. Only then start changing things.
3. **Good variables to test, in this order:**
	1. Drop time after first crack (development) — biggest effect on taste, easiest to control.
	2. Heat level in the browning phase (time from yellow to first crack).
	3. Preheat / charge temperature.
	4. Batch size (affects everything else — test last).
4. **Change in small steps:** ±30 s development, ±10–15 °C grill temperature, ±50 g batch.
5. **Same bean for a test series.** Buy enough of one bean (2–3 kg) for 6–10 roasts.
6. **Taste the same way every time.** Espresso has many variables of its own. For judging the roast, use a cupping or a French press with a fixed recipe (e.g. 15 g : 250 g water, 93 °C, 4 min), then confirm on espresso.
7. **Taste side by side** when possible: two roasts of the same bean on the same day removes much of the guesswork.
8. **Note the weather.** Outside air temperature and wind change the grill a lot. Wind is the most common reason for an unrepeatable roast.

## 6. Reading the result
| Symptom | Likely cause | Try next |
|---|---|---|
| Grassy, sour, bready, light weight loss (< 11 %) | Under-developed | Longer development (+30 s) or more heat before first crack |
| Bitter, ashy, smoky, oily surface after 1–2 days | Over-roasted / into second crack | Drop earlier, lower heat after first crack |
| Flat, papery, little sweetness, total time > 20 min | Roast "stalled" / baked | More heat in the browning phase; check wind |
| Uneven colour, some pale beans | Uneven heat or drum too full, or mixed bean sizes | Smaller batch, check drum rotation, sort out defects before roasting |
| Dark spots / burnt tips (scorching / tipping) | Too much direct radiant heat early | Lower preheat, raise the drum, or use indirect heat |
| Very fast first crack (< 9 min), harsh taste | Too much heat early | Lower preheat 10–15 °C |

## 7. Resting
- Beans release CO₂ after roasting. Store in a valve bag or a jar with the lid loose for the first day.
- Filter / cupping: taste from 24 h. Espresso: usually best from day 5–10, often good up to 3–4 weeks.
- Always note the rest time when you taste. Taste notes without rest time are hard to compare.

## 8. Safety and practical points
- Roast **outside only**. Smoke after first crack is heavy; second crack smoke is worse.
- Chaff (the thin skin) burns. Clean the grill bottom and grease tray after each session. Never leave the grill unattended.
- Do not use the drum or grill with old fat residue near the drum — fat smoke gets into the coffee.
- The drum and spit are very hot. Have a place ready to put the drum down before you start.
- Coffee beans can catch fire if left far into second crack. Keep a lid or a metal tray nearby.

## 9. Green bean storage
- Cool, dark, dry place, in the original jute/GrainPro bag or an airtight box. Green beans keep 6–12 months.
- Write the purchase date and supplier on the bag, and in the session note.
