# 🗓️ Collaboration Morning Brief — Wednesday, 21 October 2026

## 🔵 Microsoft Teams
- The Meet app in Teams is being renamed to **Events app** (now live in GCC), letting users discover, track, and plan organisational events directly in Teams; users can now use the Teams Meet app to discover events across their organization, track what important events are upcoming, and plan and organize their own professional events, and with this release, Meet app will be renamed to Events app.
- Workflow builder gets more flexible: you will now be able to add multiple steps to your workflow when building one from scratch.
- Admin heads-up: an upcoming change to voicemail message delivery by email changes how Teams voicemail messages are delivered, so organisations can send notifications from their own domain and apply SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and related domain protection policies.
- Minor service blip: on October 20, Microsoft Teams recorded 83 outage reports against a baseline of 13, and Microsoft 365 recorded 73 reports against a baseline of 22 — resolved quickly, no sustained disruption confirmed.

## 🤖 Copilot & AI in Teams
- Copilot is getting a structural overhaul: Microsoft has split Copilot into two billing tracks and rebuilt the app around a new Home screen, Code, and an always-on agent called Autopilot, with Plugins replacing the standalone Agents entry (called Agents & Skills for some preview users) in Chat and the Customize entry in Cowork.
- Billing change confirmed and delayed slightly: usage-based billing will now be enabled by default for new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business subscriptions purchased through CSP, beginning December 1, 2026 (pushed back from the earlier Nov 2 date), with a default spending limit of 4,000 Copilot Credits per user per month, adjustable by admins.
- Teams Interpreter agent is expanding: a new Microsoft 365 Copilot for Teams bilingual consecutive interpretation mode with Interpreter agent is rolling out via Message Center.
- Cross-platform AI dev tooling: multi-repo support and other improvements for GitHub Copilot in Slack and Microsoft Teams adds support for working across multiple repositories in a single conversation.

## 🔗 Ecosystem & Integrations
- Big infrastructure shift: Azure Communication Services — Microsoft's CPaaS play, the Twilio compete — is retiring as a standalone product, while Teams Phone Agent is generally available and deepfake detection is coming to Teams meetings. Reassuringly, Teams interop — Teams Phone, Teams Phone Extensibility, Unify, Teams video interop and Click-2-Call for Teams voice apps — is not impacted, though ACS Direct Routing, PSTN Direct Offer, Chat and Rooms are retiring.
- Copilot in SharePoint has reached general availability, and Microsoft is also adding a Smart Table of Contents feature to the OneDrive and SharePoint PDF viewer on the web, where Copilot can analyse document structure and generate a hierarchical, clickable navigation panel.
- Admin note: Microsoft Copilot Studio Teams app retirement continues the move from the Teams app to the Copilot Studio web app for creating and managing classic style chatbot agents.

## Meetingroom Equipment
- Logitech is this month's big room-hardware mover: on 8 October Logitech announced its next generation of Teams Rooms solutions built on MDEP, and separately the Zone Vibe Pro for Business boomless headset launched on 30 September and is Certified for Microsoft Teams, including the Premium Microphone for Open Office designation.
- Device lifecycle warning: Exchange Web Services is retiring and Teams devices below the minimum version will lose calendar sync, affecting both Rooms devices and Teams mobile (iOS 8.9.0+, Android 1416+ required); notably Google Workspace to Teams calendar sync is unsupported from October.
- Certified hardware ecosystem keeps growing across Poly (HP)'s Studio X series as the most widely deployed enterprise MTR platform, and Logitech's Rally Bar and Rally Bar Mini, popular for modular design and AI-powered camera framing, alongside Yealink, Crestron, Neat, and Q-SYS options.

## 🌐 Broader Collaboration Landscape
- Google Workspace had an active week: Google Meet now uses post-quantum cryptography for calls, starting with laptop web clients and meeting room hardware, protecting data against future quantum computer threats, and Google Chat is rolling out AI-powered meeting recaps, beginning October 16 for Rapid Release domains with Scheduled Release domains following on October 27.
- Gmail is getting an AI Inbox: Google is rolling out AI Inbox in Gmail to paid Google Workspace customers, limited at launch to users in the United States with account language set to US English.
- Zoom pushed AI note-taking cross-platform — AI Companion can take notes in meetings beyond Zoom, with My Notes working across third-party platforms like Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and WebEx — plus new translated speech projection and voice simulation features, with countdown alerts for regulated participants nearing meeting limits.
- Slack shipped incremental AI/workflow upgrades, including the ability to kick off an agent conversation from a workflow, automatically starting a conversation with Slackbot or an agent using context collected in the workflow.
