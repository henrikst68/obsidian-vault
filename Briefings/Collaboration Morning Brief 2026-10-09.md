# 🗓️ Collaboration Morning Brief — Friday, 9 October 2026

## 🔵 Microsoft Teams
- Microsoft is adding support for **third-party synthetic audio/video (deepfake) detection** in Teams meetings — certified providers will analyze meeting media and send detection signals back to Teams, with rollout starting in November 2026 (Roadmap ID 573451). Microsoft Teams will support third-party detection of synthetic audio and video, allowing organizations to identify potentially AI-generated or manipulated media during meetings, with the capability scheduled to roll out in November 2026. Microsoft itself won't perform the analysis — Microsoft Teams will not carry out synthetic media detection itself under this feature; instead, a certified provider will examine meeting audio and video, look for signs of generated or altered content, and send detection signals back to Teams.
- **Security alert:** researchers flagged a newly registered phishing domain, teams-online[.]com, that was actively mimicking the full Teams login and user interface, with Microsoft Defender reportedly showing zero detections when first examined — worth a heads-up to Henrik's security team.
- Admin-side updates rolled out this week include the ability for users to **temporarily pause all notifications** and improved **town hall audio/video offloading** for VDI attendees on Azure Virtual Desktop, Windows 365, Citrix and more.

## 🤖 Copilot & AI in Teams
- **Copilot in SharePoint and OneDrive reached General Availability** this month, letting every user create/organize content, ground questions in SharePoint data, and build reusable skills. New plugins also let Copilot in SharePoint read/write into systems like GitHub and CRM tools, with human approval required for bulk actions.
- Microsoft merged search and chat: Microsoft 365 Copilot Search on Windows and web now integrates conversational chat, so instead of finding something in one experience and starting a disconnected conversation in another, users can use search results as the basis for follow-up questions, synthesis and content generation.
- The Copilot "Cowork" model picker has expanded to include several new frontier models (Grok 4.7, GPT-6 Astra/Sol, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1), broadening model choice for enterprise users.
- **Admin action needed by Oct 25:** a new "advanced package uploads" setting in the Copilot control system will automatically allow users to upload advanced packages (declarative agents with actions, or MCP servers) unless reviewed beforehand.

## 🔗 Ecosystem & Integrations
- Microsoft officially **welcomed Logitech to the Microsoft Device Ecosystem Platform (MDEP)** on October 8, aiming to give IT teams more confidence managing certified devices across their lifecycle.
- Microsoft's October partner update highlighted a new **Frontier Partner specialization**, which validates partners who design, build, deploy, govern, and secure AI agents across Microsoft 365 Copilot, Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot, Microsoft Defender, Microsoft Entra, and Microsoft Purview, plus extended SMB Copilot trial and discount promotions through year-end.
- Google Workspace and Microsoft Teams took a step toward interoperability: built-in interoperability between Google Meet and Microsoft Teams on Android (AOSP) devices is now generally available, having previously been in preview.

## Meetingroom Equipment
- **Logitech** unveiled a new Teams Rooms hardware generation on Oct 8 — Rally Bar 2, Tap IP 2 and Tap Scheduler 2 are launching globally now, while the modular Rally System 2 Pro and RoomMate 2 Pro are slated for Summer 2027.
- **Cisco** used WebexOne 2026 (Oct 7, Austin) to unveil RoomOS 27 and agentic collaboration features; notably, through a new partnership with Google, Cisco devices now offer support for fully featured Google Meet natively, joining Webex, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom — making Cisco the most open collaboration hardware on the market. Cisco's collaboration VP also claimed Cisco is now the fastest-growing Microsoft Teams Rooms ecosystem partner.
- AVer's **MT300N** voice-tracking appliance earned Teams Rooms certification, giving admins a lower-cost alternative to in-room PCs for multi-camera PTZ switching.

## 🌐 Broader Collaboration Landscape
- **Google Cloud launched a "Gemini agent"** — a universal enterprise AI agent — at its Gemini at Work 2026 event (Oct 8), positioned to compete directly with Copilot. For connectivity, the agent can integrate with existing enterprise collaboration and development tools, including Microsoft Teams, Slack, Confluence, Git, and Jira, as well as enterprise systems like Salesforce and ServiceNow, and it can even spin up "coworker agents" with their own Workspace accounts.
- Google is also sunsetting Gems in favor of a new **Skills** framework, with Gems support for personal accounts ending in November 2026, while Workspace enterprise plans have until March 2027.
- Cisco's WebexOne announcements signal an industry-wide push toward "agentic" collaboration — proactive AI agents embedded in meeting and workflow tools rather than reactive chatbots — a direct challenge to Microsoft's Copilot positioning in Teams.

## ⚡ Action Items & Things to Watch
1. **Security:** Share the teams-online[.]com phishing domain alert with IT/security to add to tenant block lists before it spreads further.
2. **Admin review:** Decide on the Copilot "advanced package uploads" setting before the October 25 default-on deadline.
3. **Competitive watch:** Monitor how Google's new Gemini agent and Cisco's agentic Webex push position against Copilot in Teams — both are aiming squarely at the same "AI teammate" enterprise use case.
