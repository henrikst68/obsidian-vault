# 🗓️ Collaboration Morning Brief — Wednesday, 28 October 2026

## 🔵 Microsoft Teams
- Microsoft Teams experienced a possible outage Monday, with more than 3,000 users reporting problems on Downdetector at 8:45 a.m PT. Microsoft has since restored the service, tracking the disruption under incident TM1526111 and attributing recovery to automatic scaling efforts.
- Microsoft will deploy an AI security layer in Teams that reads conversation content to detect social engineering attacks that carry no malicious links or files, detailed in Message Center post MC1519866 published October 21. The feature begins Targeted Release in early November 2026 with GA expected December, requiring no admin configuration.
- Arctic Wolf has documented a recurring scheme where threat actors flood employee inboxes with spam and then pose as IT support on Teams, with more than 400 incidents found abusing the external chat feature.
- Microsoft and Zoom have announced plans for improved interoperability between Teams Rooms and Zoom Rooms via a new Cross-Platform Meeting Connect feature, though an AV consultant cautioned that "Planned isn't shipping and Microsoft itself says a guest meeting will still differ from a native one."

## 🤖 Copilot & AI in Teams
- Update: Usage-based billing is now default on for new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business licenses, while Copilot SKUs without a base-subscription prerequisite become available December 1, with current offers renamed "(add-on)" beginning October 16.
- A new "Today" agentic feature enters private preview in October within Copilot and is coming to Outlook and Teams, and now when you invoke @Copilot in Teams, the entire team gets shared context and permissions of the channel, group chat or meeting.
- Microsoft Copilot Studio now lets makers give agents their own governed Microsoft 365 identity—with a mailbox, Teams presence and Office access—so agents can collaborate where work happens while admins retain governance control.

## 🔗 Ecosystem & Integrations
- Logitech announced its next-generation Teams Rooms lineup built on the Microsoft Device Ecosystem Platform (MDEP) on October 8, including Rally Bar 2 with built-in occupancy sensors, Rally System 2 Pro for large rooms, and refreshed Tap IP 2/Tap Scheduler 2 controllers.
- Administrators can now use the Pro Management portal to monitor device health and manage inventory for both Windows and Android Teams devices, as management of Android-based Rooms, panels and phones moves from the Teams admin center to the Pro Management portal.
- Copilot in SharePoint now supports plugins connecting to systems like GitHub, CRM platforms and internal tools, plus the ability to build quick workflows for Teams chats and channels when file changes or metadata updates occur.

## Meetingroom Equipment
- Logitech's MDEP-based refresh dominates this cycle: the new lineup announced October 8 includes Rally Bar 2, Tap IP 2, Tap Scheduler 2, Rally System 2 Pro, and RoomMate 2 Pro, a high-performance CollabOS appliance driving up to three 4K displays.
- Newer Teams Rooms devices running Android 12 or 13 will have support extending into 2028, giving IT teams time to plan replacements rather than waiting for end-of-life.
- Breakout rooms now support adding a Rooms on Android system that can move in and out of the main room (GA October 2026), alongside expanded health signals for storage, calendar sync and network status in the Pro portal.

## 🌐 Broader Collaboration Landscape
- At Zoomtopia on Oct. 20, Zoom introduced a Unified Context Layer that links conversation history with documents and third-party app data, with new AI guardrails, centralised AI management, Microsoft Purview sensitivity label integration and deepfake risk detection, plus connections to AI assistants including Claude, Codex, Copilot, and Muse expected this month.
- On the Google side, Google Chat rolled out meeting recaps with Gemini, giving high-level meeting summaries, direct links to notes/recordings/transcripts, key decisions, and assigned action items.
- The Microsoft–Slack legal dispute continues: Salesforce's claim that Microsoft illegally tied and bundled its Teams application is "incoherent" according to Microsoft's newly filed UK court defence, following Slack's April lawsuit alleging anticompetitive bundling.

## ⚡ Action Items & Things to Watch
- Prepare for the AI anti-phishing rollout (Targeted Release begins early November) — review how it may interact with existing security/SOC tooling, since it activates automatically with no admin config required.
- Flag the Arctic Wolf findings on fake IT-support Teams chats to security/helpdesk teams; consider tightening external access/guest chat policies given the 400+ documented incidents.
- Monitor the Teams Rooms–Zoom Rooms interop rollout before renewing any third-party interop contracts — advice is to test now but avoid long renewal commitments without an exit point.
